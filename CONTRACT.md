# Nectar product contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Nectar is a complete subscription and
usage-based billing platform owned by the business that uses it. A US buyer of
Stripe Billing, Chargebee, Recurly or Maxio can switch to it, and a buyer
weighing Orb, Metronome or Tabs gets usage depth and billing agents too. It
manages the pricing catalog and entitlements, meters usage in real time,
rates it exactly, issues and delivers invoices automatically, collects through
the merchant's own payment processor (card and ACH), recovers failed payments,
gives customers a self-service portal, calculates US sales tax through a tax
provider, syncs the books and produces ASC 606 revenue schedules.

This is a target contract: it describes the product Nectar is being built
towards, not only what ships today. Every named clause states what a buyer gets
and the acceptance journey that proves it through a public door (screen, form,
JSON API, download, command or provider webhook), with fakes only at the paid
or network edge (processor, tax, accounting, CRM and mail sandboxes). The
[Status](#status) table at the end marks each clause `done`, `partial` or
`missing` at a named commit; [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md) lists every
clause's acceptance evidence with the same status, so a build round can work
towards it. A gap in today's implementation never narrows this destination.

## Product target

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Category | Subscription billing and usage-based billing |
| Incumbents a buyer switches from | Stripe Billing, Chargebee, Recurly, Maxio (and the usage-billing challengers Orb, Metronome and Tabs) |
| First buyer | A US B2B software or service business billing subscriptions, seats and metered usage in US dollars, run by an owner, a finance lead and an engineer, with an outside accountant |

**The whole job.** Turn commercial terms and product usage into correct
recurring charges, deliver them, collect the money through the merchant's
processor, and keep subscriptions, customer access, tax and financial records
in agreement. That means: a versioned catalog with every common pricing model
(flat, seat, tiered, volume, package, commitments, ramps, prepaid credits,
annual and multi-year terms); a metering pipeline with stable event identity;
the full subscription lifecycle; an entitlements API and SDKs; automatic invoice
runs with real email delivery; card and ACH collection through Stripe, Adyen or
Braintree; smart retries and dunning; B2B collections and cash application; a
customer portal; US sales tax through Stripe Tax or Avalara; QuickBooks Online,
Xero and NetSuite sync; ASC 606 revenue schedules; SaaS metrics; CRM contract
inputs; and move-in from every named incumbent with a reconciled shadow run.

**Neighboring products stay separate.** Nectar links to its siblings rather
than rebuilding them: [Orbit](https://github.com/just-every/run-orbit) is the
CRM that sends closed-won deals; [Platter](https://github.com/just-every/run-platter)
writes proposals and quotes; [Inkwell](https://github.com/just-every/run-inkwell)
collects signatures; [Locket](https://github.com/just-every/run-locket) manages
the contract lifecycle; [Pantry](https://github.com/just-every/run-pantry) is
the general ledger; [Easel](https://github.com/just-every/run-easel) forecasts;
[Buoy](https://github.com/just-every/run-buoy) and
[HappyToHelp](https://github.com/just-every/run-happytohelp) read billing
context for customer success and support; [Purse](https://github.com/just-every/run-purse)
is the buyer-side procurement system that receives Nectar invoices;
[Nook](https://github.com/just-every/run-nook) carries chat alerts;
[Crank](https://github.com/just-every/run-crank) automates cross-tool
workflows; [Auth](https://github.com/just-every/run-auth) is an identity
provider. Each integration is a clause below; none of those products is part
of Nectar.

**How to read this contract.** The sixteen published clauses (`NEC-01` to
`NEC-16`) are the released first edition and keep their IDs and behavior; they
are the exact rating and invoicing core the target builds on. Target clauses
extend them. Where a target clause widens a published one (for example
`TAX-001` replaces the single declared state rate with provider calculation),
the published clause still holds as written, as the provider-free mode.

## Operating rules

These rules bind every clause, published and target.

- **A fresh installation starts empty.** The owner enters the business, its catalog and its customers, or moves them in. Nothing is pre-filled with sample data.
- **Exact money.** Unit prices and quantities are exact decimals with at most six fractional places; nothing passes through binary floating point. Each invoice line rounds once to whole cents, half away from zero; tax is the declared or provider-returned amount, rounded once. The rule set is versioned (`nectar-rating-1` today) and recorded on every invoice; a new pricing model ships as a new rule version, never as a silent change to an old one.
- **Same inputs, same bill.** Rating is a pure function of recorded inputs: the business's timezone and tax basis, the customer's exemption, the subscription's dates, items, prices, commitments, credits and approved contract exceptions, and the unbilled usage events. Those inputs are fingerprinted on every draft.
- **Approval names exact inputs.** Approving a draft, a change or a proposal records its input fingerprint. Executing it recomputes the fingerprint; if anything changed, the approval no longer holds.
- **History is append-only.** Price versions, issued invoices and their lines, credit notes, payments, refunds, allocations, revenue schedules, the usage ledger and the audit trail are never edited or deleted. Corrections are new records.
- **Each period bills once; each invoice collects once.** A subscription period has at most one issued invoice, a usage event is billed on at most one invoice, and a payment attempt carries an idempotency key derived from the invoice and attempt number, so a retry, a restart or a duplicate provider webhook can never charge twice.
- **Money moves only through the merchant's processor.** Nectar never holds card or bank-account numbers, never takes custody of funds and never acts as merchant of record. It instructs the merchant's own Stripe, Adyen or Braintree account, using processor-hosted fields for payment details, and records the processor's references and outcomes.
- **Policy decides what runs unattended.** The owner sets which actions run automatically (issuing clean invoices, charging stored payment methods, sending invoices, retries and notices) and which wait for a person (exceptions, credits, write-offs, refunds and price changes above set limits). Every automated action is audited with the policy that allowed it.
- **Roles are checked in every handler.** Every server handler checks the actor's capability itself, for pages, form posts, the JSON API, webhooks and downloads alike; routes are not the boundary.
- **Agents prepare; people decide.** An agent or application credential reads and proposes within its scope. It never approves its own proposal, issues a credit, refunds, writes off, changes a price or manages access. Every approval records the person who made it.

## First release: sixteen clauses

The released first edition, kept as released with its acceptance evidence added. Each is proven by its named check
in `tests/nectar/flows.mjs` (`pnpm run test:flows`, part of `rer verify`).

### Billing flows

- **NEC-01 — Set up the business.** The owner records the legal name, billing address and email, a billing timezone, an invoice number prefix and payment terms, in US dollars only. The owner declares a tax policy for one US state (no sales tax, or one flat rate on taxable lines) with its basis, and reviews it. Invoices cannot be finalized until a declared policy has been reviewed; any later change needs a new review. The timezone cannot change once an invoice is issued. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-01 business, currency, timezone and declared tax`; finalizing refused until review in `NEC-10`.

- **NEC-02 — Keep customers and billing contacts.** Operators add customers under the business's own external id (the id a CRM or previous biller uses; usage files refer to customers by it), with a billing email, address and an optional tax exemption backed by a certificate reference, and add billing contacts. External ids and contact emails are unique; edits are guarded against concurrent changes. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-02 customers and billing contacts with external ids`.

- **NEC-03 — Version the catalog.** Operators define products, simple meters and prices: flat monthly, per seat monthly, or per unit of a meter. A price is an immutable version; a new price is the next version and earlier versions keep billing the subscriptions that use them. A retired version is unavailable to new subscriptions. Products grant named features. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-03 products, immutable price versions and meters`.

- **NEC-04 — Start subscriptions.** Operators start a subscription with an explicit billing day (1–28), a start date, an optional trial end and one to twenty plan items with seat quantities. Contract exceptions — a discount on fixed and seat charges, a one-time credit, or a contract price for one plan price, each with dates, a note and the quoted clause — are recorded as proposed and bill only after a person approves them. One customer cannot have two live subscriptions rating the same meter. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-04 subscriptions with anchor, trial and approved exceptions` and `NEC-04 a subscription with an end date still holds its meter until it ends`.

- **NEC-05 — Bring in usage.** Operators upload a CSV, or an application credential posts events, each with a stable event id, customer id, meter, exact quantity and an ISO 8601 time with an explicit offset. An id already recorded with the same values is a duplicate and changes nothing; reused with different values, or with an unknown customer or meter or an unreadable time, it is rejected and reported by row. Each import is one atomic batch with its own report. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-05 usage with stable ids and duplicate rejection`; credential submission in `NEC-14`.

- **NEC-06 — Inspect before rating.** For any month, a person sees each metered subscription's exact period totals per meter, the period's boundaries in the billing timezone, days with no usage, events that arrived after the period ended, unbilled events from earlier periods and events no price rates. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-06 missing and late usage and exact totals before rating` (23:30 on 31 July in Los Angeles belongs to July).

- **NEC-07 — Rate deterministically.** Fixed and seat charges bill for the days of the period they are in effect, outside any trial; usage sums exact event quantities per price and multiplies once; late usage bills in the next period at the price in effect when it occurred; contract discounts and credits apply as approved. Preparing again from the same inputs yields the same lines and fingerprint. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-07 deterministic rating with explicit rounding` (Northwind's July invoice totals $2,081.44 line by line).

- **NEC-08 — Schedule plan and quantity changes.** Operators prepare a change to one plan item from a date and see the affected period's charges before and after (with proration) and the next full period. A person approves that exact preview; it is refused if the subscription changed since, or if the date falls in a period already invoiced. The bill run charges exactly what the approved preview showed. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-08 scheduled changes bill exactly as previewed`.

- **NEC-09 — Draft a bill run and compare it.** For a month, Nectar drafts one invoice per subscription period that started in it and has ended, lists periods not yet ended and periods already invoiced, compares each draft with the subscription's previous invoice and the run with the previous run, and lists exceptions (missing usage, large change, unrated usage, missing billing email, undeclared tax, credits beyond charges, disagreement with the previous biller). Preparing again replaces the draft. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-09 draft bill run comparison and exceptions`.

- **NEC-10 — Approve and finalize numbered invoices.** A person approves one draft or every clean draft, acknowledging any listed exceptions; blocking exceptions cannot be approved. Finalizing issues approved invoices whose inputs are unchanged with the next sequential number, an invoice date from the end of the period up to today and a due date from the payment terms, marks the billed usage, and keeps the accepted calculation, its fingerprint and rule version with the invoice. Issued invoices never change. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-10 approval, source changes and numbered finalisation`.

- **NEC-11 — Credit and record payments.** A person issues a numbered credit note against an issued invoice for no more than it still owes, with a reason. Operators record payments received outside Nectar with their method, unique reference and evidence, and allocate them to the customer's invoices, never beyond the payment's unallocated amount or the invoice's balance. For every invoice, total = credited + paid + owed. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-11 credit notes, payment evidence and allocations`.

- **NEC-12 — Follow up overdue balances.** Operators see aging (not yet due, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, over 90 days) per customer and invoice, draft a reminder stating the overdue invoices, balances and recent payments, and a person approves the exact recipient and wording while those balances still hold. The approved reminder downloads as a message to send from the operator's own mail, and approval opens a dated follow-up task that is closed with its outcome. Sending from Nectar itself is `AR-001`. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-12 ageing, reminder drafts and follow-up tasks`.

- **NEC-13 — Leave with the data and explain entitlements.** Accountants and operators download issued invoices, usage events and subscriptions as CSV, and any bill run as one JSON document with every invoice's lines, calculation inputs, fingerprint, approval and issue events. The whole installation exports with `rer export` and restores with `rer import` into a fresh installation with every portable row, including the calculation evidence, unchanged. For any customer, feature and date, Nectar explains whether the feature is granted, subscription by subscription, step by step. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-13 exports and entitlement explanations`; the restore round trip in `tests/nectar/journey.mjs`.

### Agents, access and move-in

- **NEC-14 — Let an agent prepare the work.** The owner issues scoped credentials. With `prepare`, an agent reads records and proposes (a) contract terms from a signed agreement, quoting each clause and flagging ambiguity; (b) a new price version for selected subscriptions from an exact date; (c) bill-run usage corrections with their sources; and (d) a collections reminder. Nectar computes the invoice effect of each proposal itself. A person approves or rejects; a proposal whose records changed since it was prepared is refused. Approving a usage correction re-prepares the draft run and approves no invoice. With `usage`, a pipeline submits usage events. Credentials are revocable. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-14 agent prepares, a person approves`.

- **NEC-15 — Control access.** The owner invites finance operators and accountants through Base operator sign-in, ends access (ending every session), and is the only one who changes business settings, the tax policy, access, credentials or a move-in cutover. Every page, form, API call and download enforces the role, signed-out requests get nothing, and cross-site form posts are refused. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-15 roles are enforced in every handler` (crawls every action and every GET door).

- **NEC-16 — Move in from Stripe Billing.** Operators upload owner-captured Stripe API list responses for customers, products, prices, subscriptions, invoices and billing meters with a manifest of counts. Nectar refuses an incomplete capture, previews every record it would create or reject without writing anything, applies the exact reviewed preview as one journaled batch with its original files retained, adds nothing on a repeated apply, and undoes an untouched batch. Moved-in subscriptions arrive in shadow: they are rated and compared with Stripe's own invoices price by price but can never be approved or issued, until the owner adopts each one from a period Stripe has not billed. Once its records have been billed on, a move cannot be undone silently. **Acceptance evidence:** `NEC-16 Stripe Billing move-in with dry run, journal, undo and shadow cutover`.

### Business setup and catalog

- **SET-001 — Multiple billing entities.** One installation can hold several US legal entities (for example an operating company and a subsidiary), each with its own legal name, address, EIN, invoice prefix and number sequence, payment terms, processor account, tax registration and accounting company. Customers bill from one entity; reports and exports filter by entity and consolidate across them. Before multiple entities ship, AGENT-006 executed-order setup quotes and enforces the selected entity and processor/ledger routing, previews the complete term and applies it under the same atomic human approval. **Acceptance evidence:** Create two entities with separate Stripe test accounts and QuickBooks companies, bill one customer from each, and see independent invoice sequences, charges on the right processor account, entity-filtered and consolidated revenue reports, and journals posted to the right company.

- **CAT-001 — Billing intervals and terms.** Prices bill weekly, monthly, quarterly, semi-annually, annually or every N months, in advance or in arrears, and a subscription has a contract term (monthly, 12, 24 or 36 months) separate from its billing interval. One subscription can mix intervals (an annual platform fee billed in advance with monthly usage in arrears), and new items co-term with the existing term end. Before in-advance or nonmonthly plans ship, AGENT-004 must replay their actual saved service intervals and full contract-term history with the same per-invoice/cohort engine deltas, current signed terms, cited notices and exact approved scheduling. Before in-advance or nonmonthly plans ship, SUB-003 cancellation uses their actual contract-term end, credits already-issued unused future service without changing issued evidence, preserves cancellation/reactivation history and respects GOV-002 approval thresholds. Before annual or other contract terms ship, CAT-007 migrations select eligible term cohorts, resolve each actual renewal date, leave grandfathered annual contracts unchanged and prove those invoices equal their prior signed terms. Before new intervals, advance or renewal terms ship, AGENT-006 interprets their quoted settings, previews every period through the actual finite term (only evergreen terms stop at the labelled 36-period horizon) and proves ordinary billing equals that preview after one governed setup approval. Before new billing intervals ship, inherit SUB-005: saves, pause boundaries and win-back discounts cover the exact quoted number of those invoice periods, with same-engine previews, approved policy and unchanged issued evidence. **Acceptance evidence:** Start a 24-month subscription with an annual $12,000 platform fee billed in advance and monthly metered usage in arrears; advance a test clock through 25 months and see one platform invoice per year on the anniversary, 24 monthly usage invoices, a co-termed add-on prorated to the term end, and identical totals on re-preparation. Before annual or advance billing ships it inherits REV-001: immutable per-line schedules and exact AGENT-002 correction parity; bill an annual $12,000 plan in advance and recognize $1,000 in each of twelve months, reconcile the deferred liability, journals, source drill-through and CSV.

- **CAT-002 — Tiered, volume, package and graduated pricing.** A price can be graduated (each tier's units at that tier's rate), volume (all units at the rate of the tier reached), package (per block of N units, rounding up) or stair-step (a flat amount per band), with optional flat fees per tier, for seats and for metered usage. At quantity zero no tier activates, the charge is zero and the breakdown is empty. For package pricing, N is any positive exact decimal up to six places and the number of billed blocks is ceil(quantity / N). Graduated flat fees apply once to each occupied band; volume and stair-step pricing use the inclusive band reached. Each line's calculation shows the tier breakdown. **Acceptance evidence:** Rate 0, 1, 1,000, 1,001 and 2,500,000 units under each model through the bill run and compare every line and its tier breakdown with hand-computed expected cents, including the exact tier boundaries. For graduated, volume, package and stair-step pricing, prove zero charges zero with no activated tier and an empty breakdown. Use a fractional package N of 0.125 and verify ceil(quantity / N) blocks, including exact multiples and a partial block. Prove each occupied graduated band charges its flat fee exactly once, unoccupied bands charge none, and volume and stair-step choose the inclusive band at each boundary.

- **CAT-003 — Minimum commitments, overages and ramps.** A contract can commit to a minimum spend per period or per term (with a true-up line for the shortfall), price overage above an included quantity, and ramp prices or quantities on scheduled dates (year-one, year-two, year-three pricing). Commitment burn-down is visible to operators and the customer. For current monthly in-arrears invoices, signed commitment windows are independent of the invoice interval and use business-local [from, to) dates aligned to the subscription anchor. A records member prepares the quoted signed clause, minimum windows, included quantities and dated price/quantity ramps; a different active approval member approves the exact subscription revision, under GOV-002. Preparation or rejection changes no plan; approval atomically installs the dated item segments, preserving immutable price versions and history. Stale, duplicate, incompatible-price and already-issued-period changes are refused. Minimum spend is before tax: recurring and metered charges after discounts, including signed unused-service credits, but excluding one-time fees, fixed contract credits and other minimum true-ups. Trial, pause and cancellation days reduce the nominal minimum proportionally by active calendar days, rounded once to cents, half away from zero. Each window settles its positive shortfall on its final monthly invoice (or the final cancellation period), only after earlier monthly periods are issued; previously settled true-ups are deducted after reactivation. Tax follows the signed window's taxable flag. Included exact-decimal units apply once per exact price and original monthly period, consumed in chronological order before overage pricing; trial/inactive events consume no allowance, unused units do not carry, and late events use only the original period's unused allowance. Burn-down shows nominal/active minimum, issued eligible earnings, true-ups, remaining amount and links to every contributing issued invoice, excluding tax and unrelated fees/credits. Under ct's 19 October ruling this completes the current operator/API doors; PORT-002 inherits identical customer burn-down and issued-invoice reconciliation before that portal capability ships. **Acceptance evidence:** Sign a three-year ramp with a $50,000 annual minimum; under-use in year one and over-use in year two; see the true-up line at the year-one end, overage lines in year two, the ramp price switch on its date, and a burn-down that matches the issued invoices.

- **CAT-004 — Prepaid credits and drawdown.** A customer can buy or be granted prepaid credits (in dollars or units) with an effective date, expiry and priority; usage draws them down before it bills, with rollover rules, top-ups (manual or automatic at a low-balance threshold, charged through `PAY-003`), and a credit ledger. Prepaid balances are a contract liability in `REV-001`. Before prepaid credits ship, their remaining ledger balances are included in the existing USE-006 operator/API views; PORT-002 inherits the same remaining-balance parity when the portal ships. Before prepaid balances ship, RPT-002 balance reports, source drill-through, CSV and scheduled snapshots equal the prepaid ledger after grants, drawdown, expiry and top-ups. **Acceptance evidence:** Sell a $10,000 credit block, consume it across two periods with a promotional grant that expires first, trigger an automatic $5,000 top-up at a $1,000 balance, and see the ledger, the invoice lines drawing credits, the top-up charge and the expiry all agree. At each grant, drawdown, expiry and top-up, USE-006 operator/API remaining prepaid balances equal the credit ledger after refresh within one minute; PORT-002 must preserve that parity before its portal ships. Before prepaid balances ship, RPT-002 balance reports, source drill-through, CSV and scheduled snapshots equal the prepaid ledger after grants, drawdown, expiry and top-ups. Before prepaid credits ship they inherit REV-001: purchase is a contract liability until consumption/expiry, and exact schedule/period journals, source/CSV and every AGENT-002 correction preview/approval reconcile; repeat the prepaid block alongside annual advance fees and monthly usage.

- **CAT-005 — Discounts, coupons, promotion codes and trials.** Beyond approved contract discounts, the catalog holds reusable coupons (percentage or amount, once, for N periods or forever, restricted to products) and promotion codes with redemption limits, expiry and first-time-customer rules. Trials run with or without a payment method on file and convert, extend or end by policy. **Acceptance evidence:** Redeem a 20%-for-three-months promotion code at checkout, exhaust its redemption limit, refuse an expired code, and run a no-card trial that ends unpaid and a card trial that converts and charges on the trial end date. A coupon restricted to the Pro product discounts only Pro's lines on a mixed invoice; a first-time-customer code is refused for a customer who has a paid invoice and accepted for a new one; an operator extends a running trial by 14 days and the first charge moves to the new end date with no invoice in between.

- **CAT-006 — One-time charges, add-ons and manual invoices.** Operators add one-time charges (setup fees, professional services) to the next invoice or bill them immediately, attach add-ons to a plan, and raise a standalone invoice for non-subscription work, all using catalog prices or an approved custom amount. **Acceptance evidence:** Add a $2,500 implementation fee to a new subscription's first invoice, attach a seat-pack add-on mid-period, and issue a standalone services invoice; each appears once, with its tax and numbering.

- **CAT-007 — Price changes across a customer base.** A commercial owner publishes a new price version and migrates a chosen cohort of subscriptions from an exact date (immediately, at renewal or at a later date), grandfathering the rest, with a preview of every affected invoice and an optional customer notice through `INV-002`. Current catalog cohorts are explicit sets of 1–200 subscription ids. A proposal can publish a new simple price or select an exact existing immutable catalog version, retaining its tier/volume/package/stair-step terms. Existing-target migrations move all matching kind/meter items, optionally restricted by source price; other items remain unchanged. Dated changes use the entered business-local date (today for immediate changes); renewal changes resolve each subscription’s first monthly anchor on or after the reference date, inclusive. The proposal freezes each resolved date and shows that subscription’s affected and next full invoice using the rating engine; usage/input changes invalidate approval, and approval applies all selected items atomically with GOV-002 separation and issued-period guards. Under ct’s 19 October current-door ruling, CAT-001 inherits annual/contract-term cohort eligibility, renewal timing and grandfathered invoice preservation before those plans ship; INV-002 inherits optional customer notice recipients, wording, amounts, resolved dates and actual delivery custody before that delivery capability ships. **Acceptance evidence:** Raise a plan from $499 to $549 for monthly customers at their next renewal while grandfathering annual contracts; the preview lists every affected subscription and its next invoice, approval schedules exactly those changes, and the bill runs charge the previewed amounts.

- **CAT-008 — Multi-currency prices.** A US seller can price in USD and in other currencies (for example CAD, EUR and GBP) with per-currency price books; each customer bills in one currency, invoices and payments stay in that currency, and reports convert to USD at a recorded daily rate. **Acceptance evidence:** Bill a Canadian customer in CAD and collect through a Stripe test account; the invoice, payment, credit and report show CAD amounts and a USD reporting value with the rate source and date.

- **CAT-009 — Customer hierarchies and consolidated billing.** A parent account can pay for its child accounts: usage and subscriptions roll up to one consolidated invoice with per-child sections, or each child bills separately with the parent as payer; commitments and credits can be shared across the hierarchy. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a parent with three divisions, bill them on one consolidated invoice with per-division subtotals, then switch one division to its own invoice paid by the parent's card, and see a shared commitment drawn down by all three.

### Customer record and search

- **CUS-001 — Find any customer and see the whole billing record.** Beyond storing customers and contacts (`NEC-02`), one search box on every operator screen (and the JSON API) finds a customer by billing email or contact email, partial name, external id, invoice or credit-note number, receipt number, processor payment or refund reference, metadata value (`DEV-005`) and payment-card last four digits with brand, and lands on one customer record. That record shows, for the customer and every child account (`CAT-009`): each subscription with its state and history; the upcoming invoice (`USE-006`); every invoice, credit note, payment, refund, dispute and write-off with its processor reference; payment methods on file (brand, last four, expiry, ACH bank name; never full numbers); the credit balance and prepaid credits (`INV-006`, `CAT-004`); contacts; notes (`CUS-002`); moved-in history (`MOVE-007`); and the customer's audit events. Its totals agree with the invoices behind them. Support, collections, finance, owner and accountant roles (`GOV-001`) work from it, each seeing only the actions their role allows; results never include another customer's records. **Acceptance evidence:** After a full billing cycle with card and ACH payments, a refund, a credit note and a moved-in Stripe history, search by billing email, partial name, invoice number, Stripe charge reference and card last four; each search returns the customer, and a search for one customer's email returns no other customer. The record lists every subscription, invoice, payment, refund, credit note, payment method, credit balance and audit event for that customer; its invoiced, paid, credited and owed totals equal the sum of its invoices; a signed-in support member sees it and issues a credit within their limit; a signed-out request and an accountant's attempt to act get nothing. Current upcoming composition: the customer record lists each subscription separately for its business-local current anchor period, or the period starting in `preview_period` (YYYY-MM). Each row uses USE-006’s same-engine subtotal, tax, total, review exceptions, links to its original calculation, and labels issued calculations as frozen. It never sums overlapping subscription periods into a customer forecast, forecasts future usage or prepares an invoice. Outside-service and previous-biller periods have no invented amount. The composition requires the existing customer, subscription and invoice read capabilities; other customer readers retain their existing view. This current slice does not complete the full CUS-001 target.

- **CUS-002 — Notes and mentions on customers, invoices and payments.** Members add dated notes to a customer, invoice, payment or dispute, @-mention a colleague (who is notified through `INT-003`), and see notes on the customer record and in exports; notes are append-only and audited. For existing customer, invoice and payment records, owner and finance members with the records capability add notes, accountants read/export, and agents cannot add them. Mentions name active members who can read the record at delivery time; each distinct mentioned member receives one email with the note and a signed-in record link, with provider acceptance, development suppression, failure or uncertain delivery recorded honestly. No uncertain delivery is automatically retried. Notes keep their server timestamp, author and original record identity, and corrections are follow-up notes. Completion covers those existing record kinds and roles: `GOV-001` inherits support-role note creation, `AR-003` inherits dispute notes, and `INT-003` inherits mention preferences before those capabilities ship. **Acceptance evidence:** An owner or finance member adds a note to a payment mentioning the finance lead (the support-role version is inherited by `GOV-001`); the finance lead receives one email naming the payment, the note appears on the customer record and in the customer export, and an edit attempt is refused while a follow-up note is added.

### Usage and metering

- **USE-001 — Metering API at production scale.** Product usage arrives through the authenticated API, the SDKs (`DEV-003`) or batch files, keyed by stable event ids, and is acknowledged durably before it is aggregated. Nectar publishes the capacity it has measured: at least 1,000 events a second sustained and 100 million events a month in one installation, with raw events retained for replay. The same doors accept Stripe's meter-event request body and, during a move-in, relay each event to the biller that still owns its period (`MOVE-010`), so the app's usage call never changes during or after the migration. **Acceptance evidence:** Drive 100 million synthetic events through the API with duplicates and out-of-order times, measure sustained throughput and acknowledgment latency, and show period totals equal to an independent count with no event billed twice; post a Stripe-format meter-event body twice with the same `identifier` and see one event recorded.

- **USE-002 — Billable metrics and dimensions.** A meter defines its aggregation (sum, count, maximum, unique count, latest value, or time-weighted such as seat-hours or GB-months) and filters and group-by properties (for example model, region or tier), so one event stream can price several ways. An advanced meter can be a read-only SQL query over the event store, previewed against past periods before a person activates it as a new meter version. Current native meters and the supported read-only SQL grammar compile to one exact-decimal engine. Each immutable version selects a source stream, aggregation, numeric value (quantity by default), scalar filters and up to five group-by properties. Event properties retain scalar strings, booleans, null and safe integer numbers (decimal values use exact strings); their canonical map is part of stable event identity, including CSV ingestion/export. Sum, event count, maximum, typed distinct non-null values and latest are computed over the selected [from, to) events; latest ties use the greatest event id. Missing group values group with null. Time-weighted snapshots are piecewise-constant per chosen entity, with zero before the first snapshot, the last preceding snapshot as context and a filtered-out snapshot resetting state to zero; each snapshot changes its own dimensions without future leakage. Hours divide elapsed milliseconds by 3,600,000; billing-period months divide by the actual elapsed period, including daylight-saving changes. Trial, paused and canceled service intervals contribute zero. Quantities retain an exact rational through simple, graduated, volume, package and stair-step pricing and included-unit subtraction; the six-place quantity is display only, and each money line rounds once half away from zero. Existing dated items and approved unit overrides select their actual active intervals. A source belongs to one customer subscription at a time: that subscription can price several metrics and disjoint dimension selections, but overlapping equal version/dimension selections and competing subscription ownership are refused at creation, add-on and reactivation doors. All raw source events are consumed once on their owning invoice, with selected and predecessor ids retained in immutable line evidence; source reconciliation still applies once to the raw stream. Preview, usage inspection, upcoming calculations, issued invoices, report drill-down and CSV preserve the same source properties and metric versions. Owner/finance records members prepare and activate versions; accountants and read credentials may preview, usage credentials only ingest. Activation needs saved, unchanged previews of three distinct past months for one customer; activation recomputes their fingerprints and guards the source atomically. Prices bind exact active versions and every group-by value; a new version never rewrites existing prices or invoices. SQL supports one aggregate over projected `usage_events`, optional group-by properties, `SUM` (including arithmetic and `CASE WHEN`), `COUNT(*)`, `MAX`, `COUNT(DISTINCT properties.key)`, `LATEST(quantity)` and `TIME_WEIGHTED(quantity, 'hour'|'month'[, properties.entity])`; predicates support `= != < <= > >= IN NOT AND OR`, and arithmetic uses quantity or numeric properties. Caller SQL is parsed, never executed against D1; writes, multiple statements, joins, subqueries, other tables and unknown syntax are refused. Exact negative results remain visible in metric previews; billing records a blocking `metric_negative_quantity` with the exact signed quantity and refuses approval until corrected, without clamping or issuing an ungoverned credit. Under ct's 19 October ruling, late nonadditive events are named blocking corrections rather than standalone re-aggregation/overcharging; USE-004 inherits their historical delta and governed credit-note policy before that correction capability ships. **Acceptance evidence:** Define sum, count, max, unique-users and GB-month meters over one event stream with a `model` property, price two models differently, and compare each period total with an independent computation over the raw events; define a SQL meter, preview it on three past months, and see a write statement refused.

- **USE-003 — Bulk usage from storage and warehouses.** Usage loads from files dropped in Amazon S3 or Google Cloud Storage and from scheduled Snowflake or BigQuery queries, with the same identity, duplicate and rejection rules as the API, and a per-load report. **Acceptance evidence:** Drop a 10-million-row Parquet file in an S3 test bucket and schedule a BigQuery query; re-delivering the same file changes nothing, a malformed row is reported, and totals match the source counts.

- **USE-004 — Usage corrections after billing.** A late or corrected event that touches an already issued invoice follows the business's policy: bill it in the next period, raise a correcting invoice, or issue a credit note, always with a line naming the original period and event ids. Backfilling a historical range replays rating without changing any issued invoice. The read-only historical preview accepts an inclusive range of 1–36 issued monthly periods and up to 10,000 distinct original void ids plus 10,000 distinct recorded, unbilled addition ids. It reproduces each original before rerating saved prices, metric versions, service windows and tax, retains predecessor context, propagates replayed commitment contributions within the selected range, and returns signed subtotal/tax/total deltas and original event citations. Missing issued periods, foreign/unavailable events, already-billed additions or irreproducible saved evidence are refused. The preview is explicitly unresolved and covers only the selected range: later periods may also be affected. It changes no event, invoice, credit, proposal, approval, message or existing issue block. Financial preparation separately replays the complete issued chain through its latest period, without the exploratory preview's 36-period bound. An append-only proposal freezes the source basis, per-period before/after subtotal, tax and revenue effects, policy, recipient and customer explanation. Approval revalidates that complete basis and atomically appends the effective replay, financial documents and one message reservation; stale, duplicate and unauthorized decisions change nothing. The governed policy follows GOV-002 preparation/approval; credits above its threshold require a human financial preparer (or explicit human authorization of agent preparation) and a different active owner/finance approver. Under ct's 20 October 16:55Z ruling, negative deltas always issue numbered credit notes: next-invoice policy carries them to later invoices; the other policies offset the unpaid original and carry any excess, including paid-customer liability. Applications occur once after the later invoice total, with a cited informational line; they never reduce original invoice totals, create negative invoices, change tax/revenue again or claim cash refunds. Positive deltas use the next ordinary invoice or a separately reviewed correcting invoice. A stale unissued correcting draft can be replaced against current available credit without changing its approved correction or message. Operator/customer balances, scoped tax/revenue/credit-liability reports, source drill-through, usage CSV and frozen monthly report snapshots agree. Generated customer wording follows the recorded language; authored explanations remain unchanged. Message custody permits one provider attempt, labels acceptance honestly and never retries uncertainty. - **USE-005 — Usage alerts and threshold invoicing.** Operators and customers set usage and spend thresholds per customer, meter or commitment; crossing one fires a signed webhook (`DEV-001`), an email and an in-app alert, and can issue a threshold invoice mid-period (bill when accrued charges reach $X) or apply an entitlement change (`ENT-002`). **Acceptance evidence:** Set a $5,000 threshold invoice and an 80% commitment alert, stream usage past both, and see one webhook and email per crossing, a mid-period invoice charged once, and the remaining period billing only the rest. - **USE-006 — Live usage and the upcoming invoice.** At any moment the API, the operator screens and the customer portal show current-period usage per meter, credits remaining and an upcoming-invoice preview computed by the same rating engine, refreshed within one minute of ingestion. - **USE-007 — Reconcile usage with its source.** Nectar compares daily ingested counts and totals with the totals the sending system reports (a control total per day and meter sent with each batch or through the API), flags gaps and surpluses before the bill run, and blocks issue of affected invoices until a person resolves or acknowledges them. **Acceptance evidence:** Send control totals that disagree with ingested events for two days; the bill run lists both as blocking exceptions naming the day, meter and difference, and resolving them by replay clears the block.

### Subscription lifecycle

- **SUB-001 — Lifecycle states driven by dates and payments.** Subscriptions move through trialing, active, past due, unpaid, paused, canceled and ended on a schedule and on payment outcomes, without a person running anything; each transition is recorded with its cause and fires a webhook. **Acceptance evidence:** With a Stripe test clock, run a card trial to active, fail its renewal payment to past due, exhaust dunning to unpaid, pay to recover to active, and cancel to ended; read the state history and the webhook for each step.

- **SUB-002 — Immediate and scheduled plan changes with proration policy.** Besides scheduled changes (`NEC-08`), an upgrade or downgrade can take effect immediately or at period end, with the business's proration policy (prorate and invoice now, prorate on the next invoice, or no proration), and in-advance plans charge or credit the difference at once. **Acceptance evidence:** Upgrade an annual in-advance plan mid-term with "invoice now", downgrade another at period end, and change a third without proration; each invoice or credit matches its preview and the payment collects through `PAY-003`.

- **SUB-003 — Pause, resume and cancel.** A subscription can pause (no charges, access per policy) and resume on a date, or cancel immediately (with a prorated credit or refund per policy) or at term end, with a recorded reason; a canceled subscription can reactivate within a set window. For today’s monthly in-arrears plans, owner/finance approvers record business-local [from, resume) pause dates and choose retained or suspended plan access; paused service and usage charge zero, earlier unbilled charges/usage and contract credits wait for resumed billing. An early dated resume appends history. Immediate cancellation records its effective date (today by default); term-end cancellation uses the next monthly anchor boundary. The owner’s separately approved policy chooses an explicit negative unused-recurring-service credit on the final invoice or no such credit, and a 0–3650-day inclusive reactivation window (defaults: credit, 30 days). A cancellation freezes its policy. The credit is the difference between exact earned and full-period recurring lines, including discounts, with tax calculated on the signed lines; usage after cancellation is recorded at zero. Above GOV-002’s credit threshold a different person must approve and issue that invoice. Changes affecting an already-issued period are refused; issued evidence stays unchanged. Reactivation appends history, checks current meter ownership, and bills only active days outside the recorded gap. Agents/accountants cannot change lifecycle or policy. Under ct’s 19 October ruling this completes current doors; CAT-001 inherits nonmonthly term boundaries and cancellation of already-issued future service, while INV-005, INV-006 and PAY-007 retain governed refunds/customer-balance handling before those capabilities ship. **Acceptance evidence:** Pause for two months and resume, cancel immediately with a prorated credit, cancel another at term end, and reactivate a third; invoices, credits, entitlements and state history agree for each.

- **SUB-004 — Renewals and renewal notices.** Term subscriptions renew automatically or end, with optional price uplift at renewal, and Nectar sends renewal notices on the schedule the business sets, including the notices US state automatic-renewal laws such as California's require for consumer and small-business plans. Following California's Automatic Renewal Law (Business and Professions Code §17600 and following), a customer who signed up online can cancel online with no call, chat or extra retention step required beyond one optional save offer (`SUB-005`), and a term of one year or longer that renews automatically gets a notice 15 to 45 days before renewal stating the renewal date, price and how to cancel. Current term configuration is a GOV-002 saved proposal for an existing live subscription, approved by a different authorized person (or the explicit audited sole-owner confirmation where GOV-002 allows it). Terms are 1–120 whole months, separate from monthly in-arrears billing, starting on the subscription's billing anchor day. Renew/end, a 0–100% uplift, and a notice lead of 1–365 calendar days are explicit; automatic terms of 12 months or longer refuse leads outside 15–45 inclusive. Dates use the business timezone and [start, end) service bounds. Each renewal compounds simple unit prices and every model's unit/flat tier prices to six places, half away from zero, before the engine's ordinary once-per-line cent rounding; discounts and original prices remain intact, and later newly chosen item prices start their own uplift history. Daily Cron processes a boundary once and attempts one configured Base mail-provider submission on the first tick on or after the notice date before renewal, preserving the quoted renewal-date/new-price snapshot and actual outcome. Labels distinguish provider acceptance, refusal, unconfirmed outcome and mail not configured; none claims mailbox delivery or legal certification. The opaque cancellation link is scoped only to that subscription's next renewal, hashed at rest, rotated on reissue, and invalid after cancellation, renewal or term replacement. Its GET never changes state; its single same-origin POST ends renewal at the saved boundary without sign-in, call, chat, mandatory reason or retention step. The page exposes no customer identity or invoice data. SUB-005 may add one optional save; future PAY-002/PORT-003 online doors inherit cancellation requiring no more confirmations than signup. CAT-001's inherited term parity is included: AGENT-006 quotes and previews the full finite term (36 monthly periods for automatic renewal, explicitly labelled), CAT-007 resolves the actual term boundary and preserves grandfathered terms, and AGENT-004 replays a complete recorded term. **Acceptance evidence:** Advance a test clock to 30 days before an annual renewal; the notice goes out with the renewal date, the new price and a cancellation link; one customer renews with a 5% uplift, one cancels online from the link and does not renew, and that cancellation takes no more steps than the customer's online sign-up took; a 12-month term with notices configured outside 15 to 45 days is refused at setup.

- **SUB-005 — Cancellation saves and win-backs.** A cancellation flow asks for a reason and, by merchant policy, offers a save (discount, pause or downgrade) before canceling; win-back offers go to recently canceled customers. Offers are recorded and reported. Under ct's 19 October 16:25Z ruling, the existing one-confirmation renewal page offers at most one optional save and always keeps Cancel renewal available without a mandatory reason. The owner prepares an exact inactive-until-approved retention policy; GOV-002 requires another active approver, or explicit sole-owner confirmation where allowed. A save is a fixed/seat discount (integer 0–100%, 1–36 monthly billing periods, including the current unissued period), a 1–12-month pause with suspended access and no charges, or a permanent move of every matching source-price item to the exact compatible catalog target, with existing item end dates retained. Usage is excluded from percentage discounts, discounts do not stack, source/policy changes or issued periods refuse the saved offer, and accepting updates billing and the recorded outcome atomically. A declined or omitted offer never adds a cancellation step; blank reasons are recorded as not supplied. Once service has ended, an approved win-back policy selects cancellations 0 through its 1–365-day window inclusive (60 days in the acceptance), reserves one configured Base mail attempt per ended subscription, and quotes its discount and monthly-period count. A hashed offer-only link expires at the end of that inclusive business-local window, and is invalid after use or policy replacement; GET never changes state, and a same-origin POST reactivates once, rechecks meter ownership and installs only the quoted discount. This offer's approved window supersedes the ordinary manual reactivation window; inactive days stay unbilled. Mail outcomes are accepted/refused/unconfirmed/not configured, never mailbox delivery, and uncertain/refused sends are not retried. Existing read-authorized members and prepare credentials see the offers report; agent credentials confer no policy or customer-offer acceptance authority. Future PORT-003/PAY-002 customer doors inherit the same one-confirmation cancellation, approved save/return terms and record isolation, and CAT-001 inherits period/discount/pause parity before new billing intervals ship. **Acceptance evidence:** Cancel through the portal, accept a two-month 50% save offer and see it applied to the next two invoices; decline it on another account and see the cancellation complete with its reason. Set a win-back policy offering 30% off three months to customers canceled in the last 60 days: on the test clock the declined account receives the win-back email once, reactivates from its link with the discount on the next three invoices, a customer canceled 90 days ago receives nothing, and the offers report lists both offers with their outcomes.

### Entitlements and developer platform

- **ENT-001 — Real-time entitlements API.** The product asks Nectar whether a customer has a feature and what limit applies (boolean, numeric limit or metered quota); answers reflect the subscription's current state within seconds of a change, are served at the edge with a p95 under 50 ms, and are explainable step by step (`NEC-13`). - **ENT-002 — Quotas and enforcement policy.** A metered entitlement carries a soft limit (warn), a hard limit (deny) or an overage policy (allow and bill), and past-due or unpaid states can restrict features after a grace period the business sets. Quotas use exact recorded meter aggregation in the subscription’s anchored billing period; reaching the limit (used ≥ limit) warns for soft, denies for hard, and allows for overage. The quota is an access threshold, not a free monetary allowance: catalog prices and recorded contract terms still determine billing. Outstanding issued subscription invoices restrict this feature on the business-local date due date + the configured number of calendar days, including that boundary; zero outstanding balance restores only access still permitted by quota and lifecycle. Blank grace disables payment-based restriction, and no meter means no usage limit. Each subscription/product grant is evaluated independently; any permitted grant allows the feature. Owner-prepared policy changes inherit GOV-002 exact saved values, independent approval or explicit single-owner confirmation, stale/duplicate refusal and audit, and GOV-007 fresh-factor confirmation when required. CAT-001 inherits actual nonmonthly quota boundaries and SUB-001/PAY-004 inherit recorded payment-state parity before those capabilities ship. **Acceptance evidence:** Exhaust a 10,000-call quota under each policy; the entitlement API warns, denies or allows and bills accordingly; an unpaid subscription loses a gated feature after its three-day grace period and regains it on payment.

- **DEV-001 — Signed outbound webhooks.** Every lifecycle event (customer, subscription, invoice, payment, credit, usage threshold, entitlement change) can go to registered endpoints, signed with HMAC-SHA256 and a timestamp, retried with backoff for at least three days, with a delivery log, manual replay and secret rotation. Shadow subscriptions (`MOVE-006`) emit the same Nectar events as they mirror the previous biller's changes, and the API documentation publishes a mapping from each Stripe Billing webhook event an app commonly consumes (`customer.subscription.*`, `invoice.*`, `customer.*`, `payment_method.*`, `entitlements.active_entitlement_summary.updated`) to its Nectar event. Before outbound webhooks ship, failed deliveries inherit INT-003 per-member email preferences, current read/action authority, exact signed-in delivery links and honest provider outcomes. Before webhooks ship, DEV-005 metadata is copied into event payloads: the relevant customer, subscription, price, invoice, payment and credit-note maps are captured with the event, so later annotation changes do not rewrite an earlier event. **Acceptance evidence:** Register an endpoint, take it down for an hour during a bill run, and see retries deliver every event once it returns; verify signatures, replay one event, rotate the secret with an overlap window and refuse a forged delivery in the receiving test app; during a Stripe shadow run, each Stripe event listed in the published mapping that the test triggers in Stripe produces its mapped Nectar event once. With finance subscribed to failed outbound deliveries, force one failure and receive one exact-item email; opted-out/unauthorized members receive none and provider custody records the real outcome. Set app_account_id on a customer and subscription, settle their invoice through an existing payment door, receive invoice.paid and assert the event payload carries their exact metadata and relevant annotated financial objects; later metadata replacements leave that event unchanged.

- **DEV-002 — Documented public API.** Everything in the product is a versioned REST call described by an OpenAPI document, with idempotency keys on every write, cursor pagination, expandable objects and a dated API version per credential. Issued-invoice lists use the immutable issuance-journal order, ascending, with 1–100 rows (default 100); a cursor binds its page size and optional customer filter to the first request's issuance snapshot, so later issues, including earlier-created or backdated drafts, appear only in a fresh listing. Invoice relationships `customer` and `lines.price` are ids unless explicitly expanded to their recorded objects; legacy scalar id fields remain available, absent prices are null, and unknown expansion paths are refused. Versions are dated: `2026-10-01` (the original shape) and `2026-10-20` (list continuations are named `next_cursor`, formerly `nextCursor`); handlers build the latest shape and an earlier version undoes each later change. Each credential is pinned to a version when issued (the owner picks; default the latest; credentials issued before versions existed are pinned to `2026-10-01`), the owner can repin a live credential (audited), a `Nectar-Version` request header answers that one call under another version, a member's session uses the header or the latest version, an unknown version is refused, and every answer names its version in the `Nectar-Version` response header. The OpenAPI 3.1 document at `GET /api/nectar/openapi` (any signed-in member or credential; never anonymous) lists every GET door and every action, with each action's body fields generated from its handler's source (`scripts/api-fields.mjs`; actions that also read fields named at run time allow additional properties). Any JSON write may carry an `Idempotency-Key` of 1–255 printable ASCII characters, scoped to the calling member or credential: the key is reserved with a SHA-256 digest of the action, version, content type and body before the handler runs; repeating the identical request within 24 hours of the first returns the stored successful result with `Idempotent-Replayed: true` and creates nothing; a different request under the key is refused (409 `idempotency_key_reused`), a repeat while the first is still running is refused (409 `idempotency_in_progress`), a failed write releases its key, and stored results are discarded after 24 hours. The replay store is operational, not a business record: archives and restores never carry it, and GOV-005 erasure redacts the erased customer's personal values from any stored result. Every other list door accepts the same `limit` (1–100, default 100) and `cursor` and returns `{data, next_cursor}`; without either it returns the whole list as before. Those cursors continue after the last returned row (its id, or the audit sequence) in the list's own current order and bind the door path and page size; rows placed before that row appear only in a fresh listing, and a cursor whose row has left the list is refused (409 `cursor_stale`). **Acceptance evidence:** Generate a client from the published OpenAPI document and run the full billing cycle with it; repeat each write with the same idempotency key and get the original result with no second record. Retrieve an invoice with `expand=customer,lines.price`: the nested objects are returned in place of their ids, and an unknown expansion is refused. Pin one credential to an earlier dated API version and another to the latest: a field renamed between the versions is returned under each credential's own version's name, and a request header naming a version overrides the credential's for that call.

### Dunning and recovery

- **DUN-001 — Smart retries.** Nectar runs its own retries for every invoice it issues, whatever the processor; it depends on no processor retry feature (Stripe Smart Retries only retries invoices that Stripe Billing issues, so it does not apply once Nectar owns the invoice and charges through PaymentIntents under `PAY-003`). The default policy makes up to 8 attempts within 2 weeks of the first failure; the business sets the count and the window, per plan or segment (`DUN-002`). Each next attempt's time is chosen from the decline code by a published, versioned retry table (for example, insufficient funds waits for likely pay days, a processing error retries within hours) and never exceeds card-network retry limits for a card. Hard declines (Stripe's hard-decline list, including `stolen_card`, `lost_card`, `pickup_card`, `incorrect_number` and `authentication_required`, and their Adyen and Braintree equivalents) are never retried on the same method: the invoice waits for a new payment method (`PAY-005`, `PORT-004`) or, for `authentication_required`, the customer's confirmation (`DUN-003`). For ACH debits, an R01 (insufficient funds) or R09 (uncollected funds) return is retried at most twice, within NACHA's limit of two reinitiations in 180 days (default window 40 days), and an R02 (account closed), R03 (no account) or an authorization return (R05, R07, R10, R29) is never retried. Retries stop on disputes, missing methods and paid invoices. Each failed invoice exposes its attempt count and next attempt time on the invoice, through the API (`next_payment_attempt`) and in the `invoice.payment_failed` webhook (`DEV-001`). - **MOVE-010 — Usage reaches exactly one biller through the shadow run and adoption.** From the first day of the shadow run, the merchant's app sends every usage event once, to Nectar only (the API, the SDKs `DEV-003`, batch files or `USE-003` loads), and changes nothing per customer for the rest of the migration. ### Roles, security and governance

- **GOV-001 — Billing roles.** Beyond owner, finance operator and accountant (`NEC-15`), roles cover collections, sales (read, quotes and draft subscriptions), support (find and read a customer's whole record through `CUS-001` and issue credits up to a set limit) and developer (credentials, webhooks and test mode), and the owner can define custom roles from capabilities. Support members can add CUS-002 append-only, audited notes to the records they can access, with permitted active-member mentions and customer/export projection, under the same note custody and notification rules. Future custom-role recipient checks inherit INT-003: alerts require the member's current record-read and relevant action capabilities, never a role name alone. Future custom-role metadata checks inherit DEV-005 underlying-record read/write visibility; the general read capability never widens a scoped role to unrelated records. Before support or custom roles ship, inherit AGENT-007 cited billing answers under each underlying record capability: support/custom readers reach only permitted customer/invoice sources, removed authority applies on the next request, and credit suggestions require proposal capability without granting approval. Exact semantics: capabilities are named record reads (customers, catalog, subscriptions, invoices, payments, reports) and actions (records, usage, approve, receivables, export, propose, notes, draft, bounded credits, credentials, settings); the full read held by owner, finance, accountant and developer covers every family plus the remaining operator records (usage, bill runs, proposals, policies, move-in, privacy and audit). Collections holds customer, invoice and payment reads, receivables (recording and allocating payments, reminders, transfer cadences and follow-up tasks) and notes. Sales holds customer, catalog and subscription reads and draft: it prepares plan changes, amendments and executed-order setups (`AGENT-006`), each a priced preview that waits for a different approver, and those previews are its quotes until a quote record ships. Support holds the `CUS-001` record reads (customers, subscriptions, invoices, payments), notes and credits up to the support credit limit: one direct credit note at or below the limit, never a saved request, refused above it; the limit is part of the `GOV-002` credit policy, zero until the owner prepares a change and a different approver applies it. Developer holds full read and credentials (issue, revoke, repin) and no financial action. A custom role combines only record reads, export, notes, receivables, draft, bounded credits and propose: approvals stay with owners and finance (`GOV-002`), and settings and access stay with the owner (`NEC-15`), credentials with the owner and developer. Export downloads only what the role can read; a customer record includes its own ledger summary; invoice-line evidence opens with invoice or report reads, and a report reader drills each figure to its source invoice's figures; pages show only links the member can open; scheduled report emails (`RPT-002`) and `INT-003` alerts go only to members whose current capabilities read and act on the record. A note mention (`CUS-002`) names only an active member who can read the noted record, rechecked before its email. A member's role and a custom role's capabilities are read on every request, so a change applies to the next request without signing out. Before `DEV-001` webhooks and `DEV-004` test mode ship, the developer role administers them with no financial execution; before `AR-003` disputes, `INV-005` refunds and voids, `INV-006` balances, `PAY-002`/`PORT-004` payment methods, `MOVE-006` quotes or any other new record kind or door ships, it names the record-family capability that reads it and joins the `GOV-001` crawl. **Acceptance evidence:** Sign in as each role and crawl every action and door as `NEC-15` does; each role reaches exactly its capabilities, and a support agent's $500 credit succeeds while a $600 credit is refused. The owner defines a custom "Billing analyst" role with only read customers, read reports and export: crawling as a member with that role reaches exactly those capabilities in every door, and removing the export capability from the role takes effect on that member's next request without signing out. A support member adds a note to a payment mentioning a finance lead; one permitted email names the payment, the note appears on the customer and its export, editing is refused and a follow-up is retained. A custom role with no approval/receivables authority receives no INT-003 action alert even if opted in; removing required authority takes effect before the next send. A custom role reads/writes metadata only on the records its capabilities allow, and removed authority takes effect on its next metadata request. Before support or custom roles ship, inherit AGENT-007 cited billing answers under each underlying record capability: support/custom readers reach only permitted customer/invoice sources, removed authority applies on the next request, and credit suggestions require proposal capability without granting approval.

- **GOV-002 — Approval policies and separation of duties.** The owner sets amount limits and two-person rules: credits, refunds and write-offs above a threshold, price changes and policy changes need a second approver who did not prepare them. For policy changes, the owner prepares the exact values and a different active owner or finance member with approval capability confirms them. When there is one owner and no other active owner or finance approver, that owner may apply the saved change after an explicit second confirmation; policy history and audit label this **single-approver**. Preparation never changes the active policy, and approval refuses if the underlying policy changed. A first catalog price (v1) is setup, not a price change. Every v2+ catalog revision is a saved request whose exact price terms require a different authorized approver, or the same explicit single-approver confirmation when no other active approver exists. Completion is measured across the capabilities that exist: current credit-note, price-migration, contract-price-override, scheduled-price-switch, catalog-revision and policy-change doors must all conform. Refunds in `INV-005` and `INV-006`, cash-balance returns in `PAY-007`, write-offs in `AR-004`, and every future capability, provider, record kind or door inherit the amount-limit and separate-approver obligation before shipping; their own clauses and acceptance journeys carry it. This completion rule does not certify an unbuilt refund or write-off capability. **Acceptance evidence:** Set a $1,000 two-person limit; a $1,500 credit prepared by one operator cannot be approved by the same person and issues after a second approver; a $900 credit needs one.

- **GOV-003 — Single sign-on and provisioning.** Members sign in through SAML or OIDC (Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace, JumpCloud or [Auth](https://github.com/just-every/run-auth)), are provisioned and removed by SCIM, and the identity provider's multi-factor result satisfies `GOV-007`. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure an Okta developer tenant; a SCIM-created user signs in by SAML with the mapped role, and deprovisioning ends their sessions within a minute; with `GOV-007` required, an assertion without a multi-factor authentication context is refused.

- **GOV-007 — Required multi-factor sign-in.** Without any single sign-on, the owner can require a second factor for the whole installation or for chosen roles (by default every role that can refund, credit, write off, issue invoices, change prices, manage access, connect or rotate processor, tax, accounting or mail credentials, or run a move-in cutover; today that is Owner, Finance, Support and Developer, with a custom role holding bounded credits following Support; Accountant, Collections, Sales and Custom only when chosen). Members enroll an authenticator app (TOTP: RFC 6238, SHA-1, six digits, 30-second steps, a code accepted one step either side of the installation clock and each step at most once) or a passkey (WebAuthn, ES256 or RS256 keys; any attestation format is accepted and Nectar never claims to have checked the authenticator's maker) and receive ten one-time recovery codes, shown once and kept only as SHA-256 hashes. Authenticator-app secrets are never encrypted with the session secret: Base at Nectar's pin has no at-rest sealing door, so they are sealed (AES-256-GCM under a key derived by HKDF-SHA-256, bound to the factor and member) with a dedicated installation secret, `MFA_KEY`, that setup generates and deploy keeps as a secret. Archives carry only the sealed values, readable only under that key; per-session checks and passkey challenges never enter an archive; a copy restored without the key (or under another one) puts each affected member back at enrollment with the reason shown, and authenticator-app setup is refused until a key is present. A member of a role that requires it cannot reach any page, form, API call or download until a factor is enrolled and presented in the current session (pages and downloads redirect to Sign-in security, form posts redirect there with the reason, JSON answers 403 `mfa_enrollment_required` or `mfa_required`); while held they can only enroll and present their own factor, and adding a factor beside a working one needs the current session's check. Refunds, write-offs, credits above the support limit, credential changes, access changes and cutovers ask for the factor again when the last check is older than 15 minutes (a check exactly 15 minutes old still counts), measured on the installation clock. Today that is every credit note above the owner-set support credit limit however it is issued (the limit is zero until set, so by default every credit), preparing or applying a change to that limit (`GOV-001`), giving or ending access, changing the requirement, resetting a member's factor, removing one's own factor, issuing, revoking or repinning credentials, and adopting a moved-in subscription (the cutover); refunds and voids (`INV-005`, `PAY-004`), write-offs (`AR-004`), processor, tax, accounting and mail credentials (`GOV-006`, `PAY-001`, `TAX-001`, `BOOK-001`, `INV-002`) and the live cutover (`MOVE-006`) inherit the same step-up before they ship. Five wrong codes within 15 minutes pause a member's checks for 15 minutes. Removing or resetting a member's factor ends all their sessions, and every enrollment, removal, reset, check, step-up and refused code is audited (`GOV-004`). Agent and application credentials (`NEC-14`) cannot perform these actions at all. **Acceptance evidence:** The owner turns on "require multi-factor" for finance operators; a finance operator signed in by email link is held at enrollment and every door (page, form post, JSON API, download) refuses them until they enroll a TOTP factor and present a code; a refund approval 20 minutes later asks for a fresh code and is refused with a wrong one; the owner then requires it installation-wide and an accountant is held the same way; enrolling a passkey works in a WebAuthn virtual authenticator; resetting the operator's factor ends their open sessions at once; the audit trail lists each step. A copy without `MFA_KEY` holds members at enrollment with the reason shown, and the same archive restored under the original key opens the sealed secret again.

- **GOV-004 — Durable audit trail.** Every write records the actor (person, credential or automated policy), time, action, subject and detail in the same atomic batch as the change; the owner and accountant read the trail, it travels in `rer export`, and it is never edited. **Acceptance evidence:** Perform each billing action through the API and forms, read `/app/audit` and the issued invoice's audit events, and find `nc_audit` complete in the exported archive.

- **GOV-005 — Personal-data erasure with financial retention.** A request is verified before anything is erased. Owner or finance records it; Nectar sends a single-use confirmation link to the billing email on file, never an address supplied as a claimed sender. Only its SHA-256 hash is stored; it expires at seven elapsed days. GET/HEAD only show the confirmation; an exact-origin POST consumes the link. A changed customer revision/email invalidates it. Provider acceptance is labelled accepted, never delivered or verified. Only the owner approves a verified request, with a separate explicit confirmation; finance, accountants and agent credentials never approve. When the billing mailbox is unreachable or disputed, only the owner may verify by another method, recording a reason and evidence file. The owner can inspect that private file before approval; approval discards its bytes, retains only digest/size and the reason with personal values redacted, and states “verification file withdrawn after erasure” on the receipt (ct, 19 October 21:20Z and 23:45Z). Request, verification, approval and removal are audited. Approval anonymizes the customer and every existing customer-owned contact, note, metadata revision, subscription/contract/lifecycle, usage, certificate, proposal, reminder and frozen report/calculation copy; retained import originals, staging, source payloads and reversal before-images are erased through import custody. Audit keeps opaque identifiers/actions and redacts personal values. An exact generation/image fence refuses concurrent changes; erased records cannot accept new personal writes or revive customer links/sends. Required financial custody keeps only the legal label, identifiers, amounts, original tax facts and dates, for seven calendar years from the latest saved issue/payment date, then removes the financial documents and legal label with a removal receipt. Anniversaries clamp to the month's last valid day; the anniversary is evaluated in the business timezone. Current invoice issue snapshots its legal label; legacy invoice labels are explicitly identified as the label displayed at erasure, not a reconstructed historical identity. For moved-in documents whose issue/payment dates were never recorded, the receipt honestly identifies that missing evidence and uses approval's business-local date as a conservative seven-year anchor. Owner-approved retention is swept daily with active-owner authority checked in the removal transaction; the owner can also run the due sweep. Every receipt lists changed record kinds/fields/counts, the separate import-custody receipt, verification method/evidence disposition, retained exceptions and expiry, and subsequent removal. Current/future archives omit erased personal data; a destination or trusted later journal refuses older pre-erasure archives, while already downloaded external copies cannot be recalled. PORT-001 inherits the signed-in customer request door before its portal ships, with equivalent verification, owner approval, erasure and receipt acceptance. ## Push further with agents

Agents serve the whole billing job beyond today's billing and collections

prepare every customer-facing change, while people authorize anything that
changes money, terms or access. Agents act through scoped credentials
(`NEC-14`); every finding cites immutable evidence and every proposal shows its
computed effect before approval.

- **AGENT-001 — Continuous revenue assurance.** An agent continuously compares signed terms (`CRM-002`), raw usage and its source control totals, rating output, entitlements, issued invoices and settled payments for every customer. It opens a case for each mismatch (under-billing, over-billing, access without payment, payment without access, a commitment not trued up, a credit never applied), assigns an owner and pursues it to resolution, re-checking until the records agree. Amendment and unbilled-usage explanations are generated from real before/after engine calculations, including metric source properties, exact input fingerprints and cited terms/events; they never forecast unrecorded usage. ## Honest boundaries

What Nectar does not do, and how a buyer gets that job done. These are product
lines, not gaps in the target.

- **Card and bank data, acquiring and money movement.** Nectar is not a payment processor, card vault or money transmitter. Stripe, Adyen or Braintree (the merchant's own account) holds card and bank data, moves funds and pays out; processor-hosted fields keep Nectar out of card-data scope (the merchant's PCI responsibility becomes the processor's SAQ A path), and Nectar records the processor's references.
- **Merchant of record and tax filing.** The business stays the seller. Sales-tax registration and filing are done by the business or bought from its tax provider (Avalara Returns, Stripe Tax's filing partners, TaxJar AutoFile, Anrok); Nectar supplies the calculations and reports. Sellers wanting a merchant of record use one (Paddle, Stripe's managed payments) alongside Nectar's records.
- **The general ledger and audit opinions.** Nectar produces revenue schedules and journal entries; the ledger is Pantry, QuickBooks Online, Xero, NetSuite or Sage Intacct, and the business's accountant or auditor signs off ASC 606 policies and statements.
- **Sales, proposals, signatures and contract management.** CRM is Orbit, Salesforce or HubSpot; proposals and quotes are Platter or PandaDoc; signatures are Inkwell or Docusign; contract lifecycle is Locket. Nectar reads their outputs (`CRM-001`, `CRM-002`).
- **Debt collection and legal action.** Nectar sends reminders the business authorizes; third-party collection agencies, legal demands and court action stay with the business and its counsel. Delivery evidence and a reminder do not prove collection.
- **Revenue financing and payments acceptance for storefronts.** Financing against recurring revenue (Capchase, Pipe) is a partner choice; one-off ecommerce checkout for physical goods is [Stall](https://github.com/just-every/run-stall)'s job.
- **Jurisdiction.** The US is the first market: USD by default, US sales tax, ACH, US state renewal-law notices and US business norms. Multi-currency billing (`CAT-008`) serves US sellers with foreign customers; non-US VAT/GST registration, e-invoicing mandates and local payment schemes are reached through the processor and tax provider and are not claimed until tested.
- **Capacity.** Nectar claims only the capacity it has measured (`USE-001`, `OPS-001`); very high-volume raw event warehousing beyond that stays in the buyer's own storage, loaded through `USE-003`.

## Decided defaults

- **Name:** Nectar (owner-adopted 10 October 2026; `run-nectar.com` registered 14 October).
- **Scope:** the full subscription and usage billing job for one business with one or more US entities, through the merchant's own processor, tax provider and ledger.
- **Jurisdiction and currency:** US and USD first, with US sales tax through a provider; other currencies for US sellers' foreign customers.
- **Move-in order:** Stripe Billing first, then Chargebee, Recurly and Maxio, then Orb and Metronome; an existing merchant of record stays in place.
- **Build order:** the provider-free rating and invoice loop (done); shadow migration (done for Stripe files); then Stripe collection, invoice delivery, automatic runs and dunning; then the portal, tax and accounting sync; then pricing breadth, revenue recognition, metrics and the remaining move-ins; agents throughout.
- **Billing timing:** legacy monthly arrears keep their saved calculation versions. CAT-001 calendars separate service spans, invoice due dates and finite contract terms, clamp full anniversary anchors at month ends, bill fixed/seat prices in advance or arrears and meter only completed consumption intervals. An issued component is claimed once; a new co-termed add-on receives its own prorated component. A finite term ends unless a governed renewal extends it.
